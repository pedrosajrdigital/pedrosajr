Bongas (by Enolgas): more price requests through digital

Demand generation Ads CRM and data

Digital presence organized to generate price requests in a measurable way, at a fluid automation company from the Enolgas group: a new website, campaigns by product and service, and monthly tracking of leads, price requests and new customers.

What was done

  • New website, live in October 2025, and a review of all promotional material (catalogs, leaflets, banners).
  • Campaigns oriented to products and services and, in 2026, a new ad structure: several ads for different audiences and segments, reaching a wider range of potential customers.
  • Reseller Acceleration project: email marketing and a campaign restricted to the reseller list. In June, 6 conversions, all turned into price requests.
  • Monthly tracking of qualified leads, price requests, new customers and revenue from digital origin, with CRM adjustments under way.

Result in the first half of 2026

Qualified leads more than doubled and price requests via digital went from 229 to 476. Counting recurring customers who came from digital as well, the share of these customers in the company's total revenue went, as an annual average, from 5% to 20%. The 5% corresponds to the period before the active campaigns, when ads were static and brought few leads. In the direct comparison between the first half of 2025 and of 2026, the rise was from 11% to 20%. With about R$ 36 thousand invested in ads over the half-year, the average cost comes to around R$ 76 per digital price request, and digital accounts for about 25% of all price requests.

What has not worked yet

  • In March, an ad clean-up requested by the sales team to cut the roughly 500 monthly unqualified contacts also reduced valid price requests: the March peak of 108 was followed by a drop to 64 in June. The recommendation is to bring back the broader-reach ads and monitor over the following months.
  • Failures in the automatic payment of ads took campaigns offline for several days.
  • Inconsistencies in CRM data make it harder to connect customer service records to the ad system, and leads received outside business hours or on weekends are classified as unqualified even when they could become price requests.

Mercedes-Benz

Account recovery Commercial negotiation

Mercedes-Benz had cut its purchases from the supplier from 100% to 5% of demand, shifting its focus to other brands. The task: rebuild that relationship. The diagnosis pointed to a product 50% more expensive than the competition's and a lack of technical support for dealerships. Both points were fixed and negotiations resumed at several levels of the customer. Share went from 5% to 30% in the first months and reached 50% after about six months of direct follow-up.

Mahle Group

Profitability Cost reduction

The unit was running with idle processes and a pricing policy out of touch with the market. Every cost was reviewed, the prices in use renegotiated and what had no return was cut. Result: more than 30% increase in the unit's net profitability.

Ford — Fiesta / EcoSport

Technical failure Recall avoided

After the launch of the Fiesta and the EcoSport, a bearing on the secondary shaft began to fail. Ford wanted the supplier to bear the cost of a recall. Days on a test bench followed until the real cause was found: the aluminum housing was not rigid enough in one specific area, which deformed the bearing during assembly. Instead of redesigning the whole part — expensive and slow — the proposed solution was a steel insert only at that point. It worked, avoided the recall, and the supplier was even chosen to manufacture the insert.
